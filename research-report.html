Vegetation cover and modeled urban heat in Budapest

A descriptive comparison of the six least-vegetated and six most-vegetated districts using values reported for all 23 Budapest districts in a 2023 environmental-climate analysis.

Status: Not peer-reviewed; no original environmental field measurements yet.

This brief is an AI-assisted secondary analysis of published values. It does not investigate disease, measure temperatures, or demonstrate a causal effect of vegetation on climate.

Research question

How different are the published district-level modeled heat-frequency values for Budapest’s least-vegetated and most-vegetated districts under two projected emissions scenarios for 2041–2070?

Method

All 23 districts were ranked by their reported share of vegetated land cover. The six lowest and six highest districts were compared using unweighted averages of the published modeled heat-frequency values. The two groups are selected using vegetation rank, not directly measured temperature exposure.

We retained two climate-model scenarios, RCP4.5 and RCP8.5, over the same 2041–2070 period. The reported heat indicator is the number of days annually when a modeled 30-year average daily maximum temperature reaches at least 30 °C—not a count of actually observed hot days in any given year.

For example, District XII's published vegetation cover is 75.2%, while District VII's is 1.1%; their RCP4.5 modeled threshold-frequency values are 5.8 and 30.9, respectively. These are two district observations, not representative measurements for every street.

What this does—and does not—show

  • The published district-level climate values differ substantially between districts selected by vegetation share.
  • It cannot establish that vegetation changes caused the differences: land cover is already an input into the climate model, while built form, elevation and other factors differ.
  • Modeled 30-year-average threshold frequencies are not the same as recorded daily temperatures, perceived heat, individual exposure, or heat-related morbidity or mortality.
  • Equal weighting of districts does not describe the experience of the average Budapest resident; district populations differ.
  • The vegetation inputs originate approximately from 2015–2017, while model outputs describe future periods. Urban land cover and emissions can change.
  • The district schematic on the website is not a geographical map or an accurate neighborhood locator.

Next: genuinely original observations

The fieldwork log supports a prospective pilot of eight selected 100-meter sidewalk segments in Districts II and VII. Students would record visually estimated tree shade under comparable conditions, after a teacher reviews the method. Until observations are collected, no field results exist. Eight segments alone would not support citywide generalizations.
