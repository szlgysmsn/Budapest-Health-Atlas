01 · MEDICINE BEHIND THE MAP

Why this is public health research

Cities influence exposure to hazards, opportunities to be active, and access to care. The medical links below are supported by international evidence—not district-specific estimates of illness.

Heat exposure

Heart, kidneys & heatstroke

Excess heat strains thermoregulation and circulation; it can worsen cardiovascular and kidney disease and trigger heat illness.

WHO: Heat and health ↗
Air pollution

Lungs & cardiovascular system

Fine particles (PM2.5) and nitrogen dioxide (NO₂) are relevant to respiratory and cardiovascular health; exposure depends on location, time and many other factors.

WHO: Air quality & health ↗
Green spaces

Physical & mental well-being

Nearby usable parks and greenery may facilitate activity, reduce stress and help moderate heat. Total vegetated land is not the same as accessible public parkland.

WHO: Green spaces & health ↗
Care proximity

Prevention & treatment

Local availability is one component of timely care, alongside eligibility, staffing, appointment capacity, transportation and affordability.

WHO: Primary health care ↗
Research distinction: An environmental exposure indicator is not a measured disease rate. Maps cannot show that residents in a particular district are sicker without appropriately collected health-outcome and population data.

03 · ACCESS TO HEALTH CARE

How close is a mapped care facility?

Explore mapped hospitals, clinics and medical offices using community-maintained OpenStreetMap location data. This is an exploratory proximity tool, not a verified directory, routing service, or official health care accessibility index.

Explore facility proximity

Load available facility coordinates. Then click a district on the geographic map above to calculate the straight-line distance from the district’s approximate geometry center to a mapped site.

No live facility records loaded.

Limitations: OSM may omit services, include closed premises, or classify services inconsistently. Distances are straight-line approximations from a district center, not resident-level walking or transit travel times. District-center distances cannot establish health care inequality.

Official source for validation

Hungary’s National Health Insurance Fund (NEAK) publishes lists of contracted general practitioner services and inpatient/outpatient facilities. These are appropriate starting points for verifying locations and service types.

NEAK official contracted providers ↗ NEAK family medicine services ↗ WHO: Primary health care ↗

For real medical treatment, check a provider’s official details and opening hours; do not use an experimental research map for emergencies.

04 · AIR QUALITY & GREEN ACCESS

The next two public-health datasets

Air pollution: PM2.5, PM10 and NO₂

HungaroMet’s official air-quality network publishes monitoring-station measurements. Station readings cannot automatically be assigned as exposure values to the entire district in which a monitor sits.

Open official OLM data ↗

Data status: official source identified; a consistently dated and spatially valid 23-district PM2.5/NO₂ dataset has not yet been prepared. No district rankings are presented.

Green-space accessibility

The atlas has land-cover measures, but not yet distances to publicly accessible parks. A future accessibility study should measure walking-network distance to park entrances, considering barriers and available entrances.

WHO methodology: Urban green spaces ↗

Data status: district vegetation cover available; walkable public-park accessibility not yet measured. High vegetation coverage does not guarantee a nearby public park.

Reproducible secondary analysis
